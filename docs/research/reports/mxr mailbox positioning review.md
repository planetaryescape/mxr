## Position mxr around a mailbox people can reuse

**Proposed positioning sentence:** "Choose mxr when you want connected providers to keep syncing mail onto your computer, where your scripts, chosen agent and mxr's own human clients can work through the same mail operations; choose a connector when using provider mail inside your agent is enough." The credible opposite is clear: a connector can be the simpler fit when someone wants in-host access and does not want to run local sync. Local storage alone is already offered by mail clients, and agent access alone is already offered by connectors and APIs. The useful distinction is the combination: a synced local mailbox, provider operations behind a common daemon, and several ways for the same person to use that mail. No "only product" claim is warranted.

The mechanism needs one sentence of plain English. mxr stores the messages it has synced on the user's computer and keeps syncing with the mail provider. The daemon handles common mail work beneath mxr's web app, TUI, CLI and agent interfaces, so another client can call existing mail operations instead of rebuilding provider and sync handling. That reduces repeated plumbing; it does not remove the work of designing each client's interaction, errors or permissions. People and scripts can use the same local state as an agent. This does not make their authority identical: agent profiles are specific, ordinary CLI calls remain CLI-origin, activity records are optional and bounded, and undo depends on the operation. ([approved positioning and D-WEB-027 at `66f36c7`](https://github.com/planetaryescape/mxr/blob/66f36c749ea693516defa7d2be672eff128936e5/docs/website-refresh/blueprint/04-positioning.md); [CLI origin](https://github.com/planetaryescape/mxr/blob/f39ae66578e3099e464967ab2f042e24b1ad8ec4/crates/daemon/src/ipc_client.rs#L76-L96), [activity recorder](https://github.com/planetaryescape/mxr/blob/f39ae66578e3099e464967ab2f042e24b1ad8ec4/crates/daemon/src/activity/mod.rs#L1-L132), [operation-specific undo](https://github.com/planetaryescape/mxr/blob/f39ae66578e3099e464967ab2f042e24b1ad8ec4/crates/daemon/src/handler/mutations.rs#L633-L726))

Real alternatives confirm the trade-off without supporting a connector takedown:

| Alternative class | What it already does well | Where mxr makes a different choice |
|---|---|---|
| Hosted agent connectors, such as ChatGPT Gmail and Claude Workspace | Search, read, summarize, draft and, in documented cases, send or manage mail within the agent host. Claude documents send approval; ChatGPT app behaviour varies by app. | mxr asks the person to run local sync and own a mailbox on their computer. Public docs reviewed do not establish that connectors are universally uncached, single-account, read-only or slow. ([OpenAI Gmail app](https://openai.com/business/plugins/gmail/), [OpenAI connected apps](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11487775-connected-apps-in-chatgpt), [Claude Workspace connector](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/10166901-use-google-workspace-connectors)) |
| Local mail clients, such as Thunderbird and Mimestream | Give people a direct mail interface and local-storage options; Thunderbird is free and open source, while Mimestream focuses on Gmail on Mac. | Local storage is not mxr's differentiator by itself. The homepage must explain the reusable agent, script and human-client runtime around synced mail. ([Thunderbird FAQ](https://support.mozilla.org/en-US/kb/thunderbird-faq), [Mimestream](https://mimestream.com/)) |
| Mail tools, such as Google Workspace CLI and notmuch | `gws` gives agents and people a JSON CLI for Workspace APIs; notmuch searches and tags locally stored mail. | They are useful precedents, not equivalent combined offerings: `gws` is an API CLI, while notmuch relies on mail being delivered or synced separately and does not send mail. ([Google Workspace CLI](https://github.com/googleworkspace/cli), [notmuch](https://notmuchmail.org/)) |

No price table is needed to make this choice. The verified material does not establish comparable pricing across connectors and tools, and each solves a different job. A few user-forum posts are only anecdotal questions about storage and searchable history; they do not establish market demand or prove a privacy advantage.

## Redline the hero support, then show one captured job

**Current candidate support:** "A Gmail connector gives your agent access to Gmail. mxr syncs connected mail into a local mailbox, where you, your scripts, and your agents can reuse its mail operations while your provider keeps syncing."

**Proposed support (47 words):** "Your synced mail stays on your computer while mxr keeps syncing with your provider. Search your local history, build a report or workflow from it, and use that mail from your scripts, agents or mxr's own clients. Sync, sending and supported changes still go through your provider."

What improves: the reader gets the ownership and continued-sync facts first, then concrete jobs and the shared use. "Same mail operations" stays out of the hero; explain it after a result. The provider boundary is explicit. What may be lost: naming Gmail in the first sentence and directly rebutting connector access. Keep that answer immediately below the support copy as a short, balanced fit comparison, not as the opening claim. The draft sentence is proposed copy, not approved copy.

**First-screen content order:** (1) locked headline; (2) locked category as normal readable text; (3) the support paragraph above; (4) one captured product proof; (5) primary CTA "Try it with demo mail," which must open the existing synthetic-mail quick start and never start a real account connection; (6) secondary links "Use it with your agent" and "See how people use mxr." Link the human route to the existing `/guides/your-day/` page. Keep an install command available for ready-to-install visitors. Show the connector answer and daemon explanation beside or just below the proof, where the reader has a concrete result to connect to the mechanism. Keep cache, attachment and provider limits next to the first claim they qualify, rather than stacking every caveat in the hero.

Use the existing S2 setup only if a corrected fixture yields different bucket counts and READ flags that make the ranking legible. Otherwise capture one non-mutating synthetic-mail search, for example: find a 2019 supplier contract by name. Run the exact search through the CLI or a script and through one existing agent surface against the same configured demo account; record the unedited inputs and outputs with matching message IDs, account and history scope, current sync state, binary version and source SHA. Label which client made each request. This shows that people, scripts and an agent can use the same mailbox without presenting an unsubscribe action as the product. If a later proof claims human review or shared draft state, S3 must first verify the same draft ID/account/revision in the agent surface and mxr's web or TUI, plus the preview or approval step. That handoff is not observed yet; do not stitch separate captures into an unobserved workflow or fabricate output. Keep the provider boundary beside the proof: sync, sends and supported changes still go through the provider.

## Let Why mxr teach the mechanism and tutorials teach the task

The first screen should answer "what do I gain over connecting mail to an agent?" in a few lines. The existing [Why mxr guide](https://github.com/planetaryescape/mxr/blob/66f36c749ea693516defa7d2be672eff128936e5/site/src/content/docs/guides/why-mxr.md) already opens with the connector comparison and provider boundary; keep that short answer, then explain what "local" means, which provider actions still need a connection, how partial backfill and cached bodies or attachments bound offline use, and how clients reach the daemon. Remove the repeated "local mail runtime" definition under its second local-storage heading, and move the provider/interface matrices below the demo path. Use one accurate diagram or command example only if it clarifies the flow: the provider keeps syncing, the local store holds synced mail, and CLI, scripts, agent surfaces and mxr's human clients call supported operations. Avoid a second homepage pitch or an exhaustive vendor matrix.

Tutorials should get the reader to a useful result. The quick start should identify demo data and account scope, give the exact command or action, show expected output, then link to the next route. The agent guide should distinguish shell CLI, MCP profiles and optional native model features, and state what requires provider or model network access. A later draft tutorial can teach review and human handoff only after the same-draft S3 check passes. The architecture guide can name daemon, adapters and APIs after the user understands what they enable. This refines the existing blueprint; it does not justify rewriting every guide before the source copy is settled. ([homepage plan](https://github.com/planetaryescape/mxr/blob/66f36c749ea693516defa7d2be672eff128936e5/docs/website-refresh/blueprint/05-homepage.md); [claims and boundaries](https://github.com/planetaryescape/mxr/blob/66f36c749ea693516defa7d2be672eff128936e5/docs/website-refresh/blueprint/02-claims-and-content.md))

### Unresolved evidence gaps

- S2 needs a fixture with meaningful differences or a different replayed task; current output is not persuasive ranking proof.
- S3 must verify same-draft identity across an agent surface and mxr's web or TUI before the site claims that observed handoff.
- Reader comprehension and conversion remain unknown. There is no controlled connector benchmark, complete pricing comparison, or competitor visual capture in this research.
- Confirm the exact first-screen CTA links and published status of every named route during implementation; do not promise instant full-history or attachment availability.

**Recommendation:** adopt the category correction and keep both locked lines. Shorten the support copy, put a real bounded result in front of the architecture, and sell the combined operating model through what it lets people do with mail they have synced. The site can ship that sourced copy before an ideal cross-client video exists, provided it does not describe an unobserved handoff as completed proof.
